# The Physics of Feel: LMUFFB Driver's Guide

> Updated November 2026 (app 0.8.007).

This guide explains what you should feel through the wheel in specific driving situations, and which control shapes each sensation.

## Part 1: Individual situations

### 1. Understeer (front grip loss)

**The situation:** you turn the wheel, but the car keeps going straight. The front tyres are past their limit and scrubbing across the asphalt.

**What you feel:** as you turn further, the resistance stops building and the wheel goes **light**. The weight falls out of the steering, telling you that more steering will not help. Unwind slightly and the weight comes back.

**Controls:** **Understeer Effect** and **Understeer Curve**.

---

### 2. Oversteer (rear grip loss)

**The situation:** the rear tyres lose grip and the back of the car rotates faster than the front. The car is sliding sideways.

**What you feel:** a clear **pull** in the direction of the slide, urging you to counter-steer, and the car's weight shifting through the rim. It warns you before your eyes see the rotation.

**Controls:** **SoP Self-Aligning Torque** (the pull), **Cornering Feel** and **Cornering Feel Boost (Slide)** (the weight), **Rotation Kick** (the first warning).

---

### 3. Braking lock-up (threshold braking)

**The situation:** you stamp on the brakes and one or more tyres stop rotating while the car still moves.

**What you feel:** a **vibration** whose pitch follows your speed:
* **High speed:** a fast, screeching buzz.
* **Low speed:** a slower judder or grinding.
* It is stronger when the tyre is heavily loaded, and fades when the tyre is light.

**Controls:** **Lockup Vibration**, **Lockup Strength** and the lock-up pitch.

---

### 4. Traction loss (power wheelspin)

**The situation:** full throttle in a low gear; the rear tyres spin much faster than the road speed.

**What you feel:** **Spin Vibration** produces a **hum** that rises in pitch as the wheels spin up. The steering only gets lighter if the optional **Wheelspin Force Reduction** is on; it is off by default because it can remove useful steering detail.

---

### 5. Slide texture (lateral scrubbing)

**The situation:** hard cornering; the car is not spinning, but the tyres are scrubbing sideways.

**What you feel:** a granular, **sandpaper-like texture** through the rim, as if the tyre is tearing at the road.

**Control:** **Slide Texture**.

---

## Part 2: When several things happen at once

### 1. The power slide

**The scenario:** exiting a corner you floor the throttle. The rear steps out and the wheels spin.

**What you feel:** the wheel pulls in the direction of the correction, and the spin hum sits on top. If you have turned on **Wheelspin Force Reduction**, the counter-steer also feels lighter than normal. That lightening can hide the pull you need, so leave it off unless you prefer it.

---

### 2. The dive (trail braking)

**The scenario:** you brake hard while turning in. The car's weight moves onto the front tyres.

**What you feel:** a **heavy to light transition**. As the nose dives, the steering gets heavier and more detailed. If you brake too hard and exceed the grip, the weight suddenly drops: a clear message that you pushed too far.

---

### 3. Snap oversteer recovery

**The scenario:** you are counter-steering to catch a slide, and the rear tyres suddenly grip again.

**What you feel:** **the snap**. The pull reverses quickly as the car swings back, and the wheel whips towards the new direction. That is your cue to centre the wheel at once, so you do not over-correct into the opposite slide. If the optional wheelspin lightening was active, the steering force also returns as the spin ends.

---

### 4. Reaching the limit in a long corner

**The situation:** you build up steering in a long, fast corner until the front tyres reach their peak grip.

**What you feel:** a progressive lightening that shows the peak of grip in GT and prototype cars alike. Tune how early and how sharply it arrives with **Understeer Curve**.
